The problem

Before a crew or a rover goes to the Moon or Mars, it practises on Earth. The practice helps only if the ground on Earth is like the ground where it will land. Which place on Earth is that?

Our solution

We measured Earth, the Moon and Mars the same way: terrain, temperature and rock. Pick a target, and the tool compares it with all of Earth's land, maps the closest places, and tests if the match is better than chance.

How to read a result

  • Every target has a best 1% of Earth. It is an order, not a quality.
  • Read the rank and the chance test, not the score.
  • A place in a list is not a result by itself.

Limits

  • One cell is 6 to 7 km across. Nothing here describes boulders or soil.
  • Rock composition is weak data on the Moon and on Mars.
  • The Mars temperatures come from a model, not from measurements.
